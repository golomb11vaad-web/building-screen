# Product Constitution - SmartScreen

> Authority: foundational
> Status: approved
> Approved by: Emil
> Approved on: 2026-06-15
> Supersedes: none

## Core Belief

People who live in the same building share more of each other's lives than
they realize - and the lobby, the one space everyone passes through, can make
that shared life visible instead of invisible.

## Product Truths

### 1. Urgent, building-specific information always comes first

Emergency alerts, building notices, and time-sensitive messages (an elevator
outage, a package room closure, a planned water shutoff) take visual priority
over ambient content like weather or news, no matter what else is scheduled to
display.

### 2. Every piece of content is about this building and the people in it

Weather, news, and other ambient information exist to serve residents' daily
lives - they support the picture of "what's happening around here today," not
generic filler. Building-specific messages (notices, events, deliveries,
announcements) always have a place and are never crowded out by ambient
widgets. SmartScreen should never feel like a screen that could be running in
any building.

### 3. Content is glanceable, never demanding

Residents pass by in seconds - often with hands full, kids in tow, or
mid-conversation. Everything shown must be understandable at a glance, in the
time it takes to walk past. SmartScreen is ambient signage, not a kiosk: it
never requires stopping, tapping, or reading for more than a few seconds.

## The Job

SmartScreen is hired to make a residential building feel like a community, not
just an address - keeping residents informed about what matters (building
notices, weather, the day ahead) while quietly reinforcing that they belong
somewhere people pay attention to.

Success feels like:

- A resident glances at the screen on their way out and feels briefly,
  pleasantly "in the loop" about their building and their day.
- A visitor or guest senses, within seconds, that this is a building where
  people care about the shared space.
- Building staff post a notice once and trust that the people who need to see
  it, will.

## Voice

- Short, plain sentences - like a note pinned to a community board, never a
  corporate memo.
- Names things directly and warmly ("Elevator B is out until 3pm" rather than
  "We apologize for any inconvenience this outage may cause").
- Avoid: marketing language, false urgency (ALL CAPS or "ALERT" for routine
  items), corporate/legal phrasing, and overly formal address ("Dear
  Residents").

## What This Product Will Never Be

- A surveillance tool - no cameras, tracking, or monitoring of who passes
  through the lobby. SmartScreen only displays; it never watches.
- A third-party ad billboard - no paid advertising from outside businesses.
  Every message either comes from the building/community or directly serves
  residents (weather, news). SmartScreen is the building's voice, not a
  revenue channel.
- A replacement for staff - never the sole channel for anything urgent,
  sensitive, or personal. Concierge and management remain the real point of
  contact; SmartScreen complements people, it doesn't substitute for them.

## Tie-Breaker

When two reasonable directions conflict, prefer the one that:

1. best serves the job;
2. preserves the product truths;
3. avoids the prohibited failure modes.
